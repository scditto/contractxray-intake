# Contract X-Ray Scoring Rubric
## Version 5.1 | February 22, 2026

**Purpose:** Comprehensive scoring guide for evaluating PBM contracts against Fiduciary-Aligned PBM Contract Standards  
**Status:** Calibrated against three contracts spanning the quality spectrum (Fiduciary-Aligned to Traditional)  
**Framework:** 10 Fiduciary Alignment and Transparency Standards (Section I)

---

## CRITICAL FRAMING: BUSINESS MODEL VS. CONTRACT TERMS

> **"We're helping employers select the right business model, not just negotiate better contract terms. No contract term can override a misaligned business model."**

A traditional PBM with spread pricing, vertical integration, and rebate retention has **structural conflicts** that cannot be papered over with favorable language. The contract analysis reveals the business model beneath the terms.

**This rubric distinguishes between:**
- **Structural Issues** (business model) — Cannot be negotiated away; require vendor change
- **Contractual Gaps** (missing language) — Can potentially be added through negotiation

When scoring, structural issues warrant larger deductions than contractual gaps because they represent fundamental misalignment that words cannot resolve.

---

## How to Use This Rubric

1. **Find the relevant contract language** using Quote Patterns
2. **Match to scoring threshold** based on what's present/absent
3. **Identify structural vs. contractual issues** (business model vs. missing language)
4. **Check Calibration Notes** to apply lessons from baseline contracts
5. **Compare to Reference Scores** for calibration consistency
6. **Document exact quotes** supporting your score
7. **Identify Negotiation/Remediation items** for gaps

# PROVISION #1: FIDUCIARY LOYALTY COMMITMENT

## Definition
PBM acknowledges Plan Sponsor's fiduciary status and commits to act consistent with duties of loyalty, prudence, and diligence, solely in the interest of plan participants.

## Model Language
> PBM acknowledges Plan Sponsor serves as a fiduciary under ERISA and related laws. In performing services under this Agreement, PBM shall act in a manner consistent with duties of loyalty, prudence, and diligence.
> 
> PBM shall act solely in the interest of plan participants and beneficiaries and shall not place its own financial interests, or those of affiliates or subcontractors, ahead of the interests of the Plan or its participants.

## Scoring Thresholds

### 90-100 EXCELLENT
- Explicit ERISA fiduciary acknowledgment
- Commits to loyalty, prudence, diligence
- "Solely in interest of participants" language
- Won't place own financial interests ahead of Plan
- Includes affiliates and subcontractors
- Forward-looking commitment

### 75-89 GOOD
- Strong conflict-free position demonstrated
- "Sole consideration" or "sole source of revenue" language
- No supply chain ownership conflicts
- Extended to family members or forward-looking prohibition
- Missing explicit ERISA acknowledgment

### 60-74 ACCEPTABLE
- No conflicts of interest stated
- Administrative fees are only revenue
- No pharmacy or supply chain ownership
- Missing explicit fiduciary language
- Missing participant-first commitment

### 45-59 CONCERN
- Limited conflict disclosure
- No explicit fiduciary acknowledgment
- Revenue sources unclear
- Potential undisclosed conflicts

### Below 45 RED FLAG
- **STRUCTURAL:** Owns pharmacies or supply chain elements
- **STRUCTURAL:** Revenue from spread pricing or rebate retention
- Conflicts of interest exist and undisclosed
- No conflict disclosure

## Quote Patterns

**✅ POSITIVE:**
- "acknowledges Plan Sponsor serves as a fiduciary under ERISA"
- "act in a manner consistent with duties of loyalty, prudence, and diligence"
- "solely in the interest of plan participants and beneficiaries"
- "shall not place its own financial interests... ahead of the interests of the Plan"
- "does not own any pharmacies or any other elements of the prescription drug supply chain"
- "sole source of revenue"
- "only consideration"

**⚠️ WATCH FOR:**
- Conflict disclosure without fiduciary commitment
- "No conflicts" without forward-looking protection
- Revenue disclosure without participant-first language

**🔴 RED FLAG:**
- No fiduciary acknowledgment
- Owns pharmacies (especially specialty)
- Multiple undisclosed revenue sources
- Affiliated entities with conflicting interests
- Vertical integration with parent company

## Reference Scores

| Contract | Score | Key Factors |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Disclosed Rx | 75 | Family extension, forward-looking prohibition; missing ERISA acknowledgment |
| Smith Health | 70 | "Only consideration," no supply chain ownership; missing ERISA acknowledgment |
| **Optum Rx** | **30** | **STRUCTURAL:** Owns mail/specialty pharmacies, vertical integration with UHC, multiple undisclosed revenue sources (spread, excess rebates) |

## Calibration Notes

**Key Insight:** Many transparent PBMs *demonstrate* fiduciary alignment through their business model (no conflicts, sole revenue from fees) but don't *state* it explicitly. The explicit ERISA acknowledgment and participant-first language differentiates Excellent from Good.

**Structural vs. Contractual:** Optum's low score (30) reflects structural conflicts (vertical integration) that cannot be negotiated away. Even if they added "fiduciary" language, the underlying business model creates conflicts that words cannot resolve.

# PROVISION #2: PASS-THROUGH PHARMACY COSTS

## Definition
PBM charges Plan exactly what it reimburses pharmacy with no spread pricing or post-adjudication value extraction.

## Model Language
> The Plan shall be billed only the amounts actually paid by PBM to pharmacies for ingredient cost and dispensing fees, with no retained spread, markup, differential pricing, or other margin.
> 
> PBM shall not assess, retain, or recover value from pharmacies or from the Plan after point of sale or claim adjudication, including clawbacks, DIR fees, reconciliation adjustments, retroactive pricing changes, performance-based fees, or similar post-adjudication charges affecting Plan cost.

## Scoring Thresholds

### 90-100 EXCELLENT
- Explicit "no spread pricing" language
- Explicit prohibition of DIR fees
- Explicit prohibition of clawbacks
- Explicit prohibition of post-adjudication adjustments
- MAC uniformity across all clients
- 100% pass-through stated
- "Any other modification" catch-all

### 80-89 GOOD
- Explicit "no spread pricing" language
- 100% pass-through stated
- MAC uniformity
- Silent on post-adjudication adjustments (no enabling language, but no explicit prohibition)
- Overpayment recovery directed to Plan

### 60-74 ACCEPTABLE
- Pass-through concept present but not comprehensive
- No spread language but with some ambiguity
- MAC methodology not clearly uniform
- Some reconciliation language present

### 45-59 CONCERN
- Weak or qualified pass-through language
- "Commercially reasonable" pricing
- MAC varies by client or not disclosed
- Post-adjudication adjustment language present

### Below 45 RED FLAG
- **STRUCTURAL:** Spread pricing IS the business model
- Spread pricing allowed or explicitly admitted
- DIR fees explicitly allowed
- Clawback language present
- "PBM may retain difference" language
- Post-adjudication adjustments enabled

## Quote Patterns

**✅ POSITIVE:**
- "does not engage in spread pricing"
- "charges Plan the same amount... as [PBM] reimburses the pharmacy"
- "no difference between what the Participating Pharmacy charges and what is collected from Client"
- "100% pass-through"
- "same MAC list(s) for all clients"
- "will not retain any portion of the difference"
- "any other modification that creates a differential"
- "recover overpayments... and direct the funds to the Plan"

**⚠️ WATCH FOR:**
- "subject to reconciliation"
- "pricing adjustments"
- "network optimization fees"
- "performance-based adjustments"
- Silence on post-adjudication adjustments

**🔴 RED FLAG:**
- **"PBM may retain the difference"** → Explicit spread admission
- **"United will retain the difference between what United reimburses the Network Pharmacy and Customer's payment"** → Optum's actual language
- "spread may apply"
- DIR fee language without prohibition
- Clawback provisions

## Reference Scores

| Contract | Score | Key Factors |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Disclosed Rx | 90 | "Does not engage in spread pricing," explicit prohibition, MAC uniformity |
| Smith Health | 90 | "No difference," MAC uniformity, explicit prohibition |
| **Optum Rx** | **20** | **STRUCTURAL:** Explicit spread pricing admission: "United will retain the difference between what United reimburses the Network Pharmacy and Customer's payment" |

## Calibration Notes

**Key Insight:** Optum's score (20) is the lowest possible because they don't just *fail* to prohibit spread pricing—they **explicitly admit it** in the contract. This is the opposite of the model requirement.

**Structural Issue:** Spread pricing is Optum's business model, not a negotiable contract term. No language change can eliminate this when the underlying economics depend on spread retention.

# PROVISION #3: REBATE & MANUFACTURER REVENUE TRANSPARENCY

## Definition
PBM passes through 100% of all manufacturer revenue with comprehensive definition and full upstream visibility.

## Model Language
> PBM shall fully disclose all manufacturer revenue received in connection with the Plan, including rebates, administrative fees, service fees, data fees, formulary placement fees, market share payments, price protection payments, inflationary credits, grants, discounts, or any other consideration of value.
> 
> Where manufacturer revenue flows through third-party aggregators or group purchasing organizations, PBM shall disclose all amounts received by PBM and shall make reasonable efforts to obtain and disclose upstream retention amounts where available.
> 
> Manufacturer revenue — including rebates, fees, alternative discounts, and other remuneration — shall be passed through one hundred percent (100%) to the Plan and shall not be retained, pooled, offset, reclassified, or obscured.

**CAA 2026 Alignment:** The phrase "rebates, fees, alternative discounts, and other remuneration" is drawn from §6701. Using this statutory language ensures the contract definition aligns with federal reporting requirements.

## Scoring Thresholds

### 90-100 EXCELLENT
- 100% pass-through explicitly stated
- Comprehensive definition (rebates, admin fees, price concessions, "any other")
- Aggregator relationship disclosed with fee structure
- Upstream visibility (manufacturer → aggregator → PBM → Plan)
- Quarterly reporting of all manufacturer revenue

### 80-89 GOOD
- 100% pass-through stated
- Broad rebate definition
- Third-party fees disclosed
- Missing aggregator detail or upstream visibility

### 60-74 ACCEPTABLE
- Pass-through with some limitations
- Definition gaps
- Third-party relationships unclear
- Basic reporting only

### 45-59 CONCERN
- **Fixed rebate guarantee only** (floor, not pass-through)
- Excess above guarantee retained by PBM
- Limited rebate definition
- Minimal transparency

### Below 45 RED FLAG
- **STRUCTURAL:** Significant rebate retention is business model
- Narrow rebate definition excludes major categories
- No upstream transparency
- "Medical Rx rebates" retained separately
- Hidden or undisclosed manufacturer revenue

## Quote Patterns

**✅ POSITIVE:**
- "100% of all rebates"
- "pass through... regardless of how characterized"
- "any other compensation received from pharmaceutical manufacturers"
- Named aggregator with fee disclosure (e.g., "Expion: 5% + $3/claim")
- "quarterly reporting of all manufacturer revenue"

**⚠️ WATCH FOR:**
- "Fixed rebate guarantee" without pass-through of excess
- "Guaranteed minimum" language
- Aggregator fees embedded vs. disclosed

**🔴 RED FLAG:**
- **"To the extent Rebates paid to United exceed the Fixed Rebate amount, We will retain the excess"** → Optum's actual language
- **"UHC retention of all medical benefit Rx rebates"** → Additional retention category
- Narrow definition (e.g., only "rebates" without admin fees, price concessions)
- No manufacturer revenue disclosure

## Reference Scores

| Contract | Score | Key Factors |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Disclosed Rx | 95 | 100% pass-through, broad definition, Expion aggregator transparency (5% + $3/claim) |
| Smith Health | 90 | 100% pass-through, broad definition; missing aggregator detail |
| **Optum Rx** | **40** | Fixed guarantee only ($160.77/retail brand); excess retained; 100% medical Rx rebates retained |

## Calibration Notes

**Key Insight:** Optum provides *some* transparency (fixed guarantees are stated) but the structure masks true rebate value. The "excess retention" and "medical Rx retention" are business model features designed to capture manufacturer revenue.

**Fixed Guarantee vs. Pass-Through:** A fixed guarantee provides a floor but no ceiling. If actual rebates exceed the guarantee, the plan never knows and never benefits. This is fundamentally different from 100% pass-through.

# PROVISION #4: DATA OWNERSHIP, ACCESS, AND THIRD-PARTY USE

## Definition
Plan owns its data, has unrestricted access, and controls third-party use with verification mechanisms.

## Model Language
> All claims data, utilization information, and analytics generated from Plan's prescription drug activity shall be the property of the Plan.
> 
> PBM shall not sell, license, share, or otherwise provide Plan data to third parties without explicit written consent. PBM shall provide annual evidence reports verifying compliance with data restrictions.

## Scoring Thresholds

### 90-100 EXCELLENT
- Explicit "property of Plan" ownership statement
- Daily or weekly data access
- Sale/license prohibition
- Third-party use restrictions
- Annual evidence reports
- Data transfer rights on termination

### 75-89 GOOD
- Strong access provisions
- Implied ownership through control language
- Protection language present
- Verification mechanisms
- Missing explicit ownership statement

### 60-74 ACCEPTABLE
- Standard data access (monthly/quarterly)
- Implicit ownership
- Basic protections
- No evidence reports

### 45-59 CONCERN
- Access with limitations
- Unclear ownership
- Weak or no protections
- No verification mechanisms
- Cost data not available

### Below 45 RED FLAG
- Restricted data access
- PBM claims ownership
- No third-party protections
- Data monetization permitted
- Critical data withheld (e.g., cost not available)

## Quote Patterns

**✅ POSITIVE:**
- "property of Client" or "owned by Plan"
- "shall not sell, license, or share"
- "daily electronic access"
- "annual evidence report"
- "provide all data to successor upon termination"

**⚠️ WATCH FOR:**
- "Reasonably requested data" (narrower than "all data")
- Access through portal only (may limit analysis)
- Aggregated vs. claims-level data

**🔴 RED FLAG:**
- "PBM data" or "proprietary information" claims
- "May use data for..." broad permissions
- No cost data available at claim level
- Access restrictions or fees

## Reference Scores

| Contract | Score | Key Factors |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Disclosed Rx | 95 | "Property of Client," sale prohibition, evidence reports, data transfer rights |
| Smith Health | 90 | Explicit ownership, protection language, annual verification; missing evidence report detail |
| **Optum Rx** | **45** | Standard portal access but "no cost info available" per broker; unknown ownership/protection language |

## Calibration Notes

**Key Insight:** The Optum score (45) is driven primarily by the functional limitation that **cost data is not available at the claim level**. This prevents the plan from assessing spread pricing, verifying ingredient costs, or conducting meaningful audits.

**Functional vs. Contractual:** Even if Optum's contract grants "data access," the absence of cost data creates a functional limitation that undermines transparency.

# PROVISION #5: AUDIT RIGHTS AND VERIFICATION WITH EXTRAPOLATION

## Definition
Plan has comprehensive audit rights with independent auditor selection and extrapolation for recoveries.

## Model Language
> PBM shall provide the Plan Sponsor with audit rights sufficient to verify compliance with pricing, financial, clinical, and contractual obligations.
> 
> Audit rights shall include access to underlying records and supporting documentation and the right to extrapolate findings to the full claims population. Audit rights extend to contracts and financial arrangements between PBM's rebate aggregator and pharmaceutical manufacturers to the extent they affect Plan pricing or rebate pass-through.
> 
> Plan Sponsor may conduct audits directly or through independent third parties of its choosing. PBM shall not restrict, approve, or control the selection, scope, methodology, or work of such auditors. PBM shall not pay for, subsidize, or provide credits or allowances toward the cost of any audit, directly or indirectly. Auditor findings shall be reported directly to Plan Sponsor without PBM pre-review, approval, or non-disclosure restrictions.

**CAA 2026 Alignment:** §6702 prohibits PBMs from paying for auditors or providing credits/allowances toward audit costs. This includes indirect payment through "audit credits" that compromise auditor independence.

## Scoring Thresholds

### 90-100 EXCELLENT
- Comprehensive audit scope (pricing, rebates, compliance)
- Independent auditor of Plan's selection
- Extrapolation permitted for recoveries
- No blackout periods or timing restrictions
- Full cooperation commitment
- Reasonable notice requirements (30-60 days)

### 75-89 GOOD
- Annual audit rights
- Third-party auditor permitted
- Extrapolation permitted
- Standard year-end blackout (industry standard)
- Reasonable access

### 60-74 ACCEPTABLE
- Standard audit rights
- Some scope limitations
- Auditor approval needed
- Extrapolation unclear
- Longer notice requirements

### 45-59 CONCERN
- Limited audit scope
- PBM-approved auditors only
- No extrapolation
- Significant timing restrictions
- Cooperation not guaranteed

### Below 45 RED FLAG
- Restricted audit rights
- Narrow scope excludes key areas
- Burdensome requirements designed to deter audits
- No extrapolation
- Audit findings limited to sampled population only

## Quote Patterns

**✅ POSITIVE:**
- "independent auditor of Plan's selection"
- "extrapolate findings to the full population"
- "cooperate fully with audit activities"
- "comprehensive audit of all aspects"

**⚠️ WATCH FOR:**
- "Mutually agreed auditor" (PBM has veto)
- "Reasonable" restrictions without definition
- Year-end blackouts (industry standard but limits timing)

**🔴 RED FLAG:**
- "PBM-approved auditor"
- "No extrapolation"
- "Findings limited to sampled claims"
- Burdensome notice or frequency restrictions

## Reference Scores

| Contract | Score | Key Factors |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Disclosed Rx | 90 | Comprehensive scope, cooperation commitment, extrapolation, auditor selection |
| Smith Health | 85 | Annual audits, extrapolation, third-party auditor; standard year-end blackout |
| **Optum Rx** | **50** | Likely standard audit rights but spread pricing cannot be recovered through audit (contractually permitted) |

## Calibration Notes

**Key Insight:** Optum's score (50) reflects a fundamental limitation: **even if audit rights exist, they cannot recover spread pricing that is contractually permitted**. When the contract says "PBM may retain the difference," an audit cannot challenge that retention.

**Structural Limitation:** This is another example where business model (spread pricing) undermines contract protections (audit rights). The audit can verify the spread exists but cannot recover it.

# PROVISION #6: PHARMACY OWNERSHIP, STEERING, AND NETWORK NEUTRALITY

## Definition
PBM has no ownership conflicts, commits to network neutrality, and does not steer members to affiliated pharmacies.

## Model Language
> PBM shall not hold any ownership or financial interest in any pharmacy, including retail, mail order, or specialty pharmacies, or in any pharmaceutical manufacturer, wholesaler, or distributor. PBM shall disclose all direct and indirect ownership or financial interests in pharmacies, manufacturers, wholesalers, distributors, or other supply chain entities.
> 
> PBM shall not engage in practices that steer utilization toward PBM-owned or affiliated entities in a manner prioritizing PBM financial interests over participant outcomes or lowest net cost. PBM shall support cost-conscious pharmacy selection by making lower-cost alternatives visible to members where available.
> 
> Affiliated entities include any pharmacy, manufacturer, distributor, or service provider in which PBM, its parent, subsidiaries, principals, or their immediate family members hold a direct or indirect ownership, financial, or control interest.

## Scoring Thresholds

### 90-100 EXCELLENT
- No pharmacy ownership (mail, specialty, retail)
- No supply chain ownership
- Explicit anti-steering commitment
- Network change notification
- Data sharing limits with network pharmacies

### 75-89 GOOD
- No pharmacy ownership
- Network transparency
- Implicit neutrality through structure
- Missing explicit anti-steering language

### 60-74 ACCEPTABLE
- Partial ownership disclosed
- Some steering controls
- Basic network transparency

### 45-59 CONCERN
- Significant pharmacy ownership
- Limited disclosure
- Potential steering incentives
- Conflicts acknowledged but not addressed

### Below 45 RED FLAG
- **STRUCTURAL:** Owns mail AND specialty pharmacy
- **STRUCTURAL:** Vertical integration with parent company
- No steering controls
- Conflicts undisclosed or minimized
- Integration pressure tactics

## Quote Patterns

**✅ POSITIVE:**
- "does not own any pharmacies"
- "no financial interest in any pharmacy"
- "shall not steer, incentivize, or direct members"
- "network changes require Plan approval"

**⚠️ WATCH FOR:**
- Partial ownership disclosure
- "Preferred" pharmacy relationships
- "Narrow network" options without choice

**🔴 RED FLAG:**
- Owns mail order pharmacy
- Owns specialty pharmacy
- "HSA requires [PBM]" integration claims
- No ownership disclosure
- Steering through benefit design

## Reference Scores

| Contract | Score | Key Factors |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Disclosed Rx | 90 | No ownership, data sharing limits, explicit neutrality |
| Smith Health | 85 | "Does not own any pharmacies," change notification; missing explicit anti-steering |
| **Optum Rx** | **20** | **STRUCTURAL:** Owns Optum Home Delivery (mail), BriovaRx (specialty); "HSA requires Optum" steering pressure; no anti-steering controls |

## Calibration Notes

**Key Insight:** Optum's score (20) is nearly the lowest possible because the vertical integration is **structural and cannot be negotiated away**. They cannot divest their mail and specialty pharmacies through contract negotiation.

**"HSA Requires Optum" Tactic:** This claim is false (HSA eligibility is HDHP-based, not PBM-based) and represents a specific steering tactic designed to drive PBM selection through integration pressure rather than competitive merit.

# PROVISION #7: CARVE-OUT AND INDEPENDENT VENDOR RIGHTS

## Definition
Plan retains the right to carve out any pharmacy benefit service and engage independent vendors without penalty or restriction.

## Model Language

**Score 5 (Unconditional):**
> Plan Sponsor is not required to use PBM exclusively and may engage other pharmacy benefit managers. Plan Sponsor may carve out specialty pharmacy, alternative funding programs, international drug sourcing, rebate aggregation, clinical management, or other pharmacy services to independent vendors at any time upon 60 days notice. Such arrangements shall not affect pricing, service levels, or guarantees for remaining services.

**Score 4 (Outcome-Triggered — for PBMs with performance-guarantee-based exclusivity):**
> Notwithstanding the exclusivity requirement, Plan Sponsor may engage an outside vendor for any service category where PBM acknowledges in writing that it cannot match a specific program on defined clinical outcome measures or total cost metrics. If PBM does not respond to Plan Sponsor's written request within 30 days, the carve-out right is deemed approved. Such carve-out shall not affect pricing or guarantees for remaining services.

## Scoring Thresholds

### 90-100 EXCELLENT
- Unconditional carve-out rights, exercisable unilaterally upon notice
- No exclusivity clause
- No pricing penalty for carve-out
- Cooperation commitment for transitions
- Data transfer to replacement vendors

### 75-89 GOOD
- Carve-out permitted with minimal restrictions
- No explicit pricing penalty
- Cooperation implied

### 60-74 ACCEPTABLE
- Outcome-triggered carve-out rights (score 4 on Issue 7.2): Plan Sponsor may engage outside vendor without PBM consent when PBM cannot demonstrate equivalent clinical outcomes or cost performance against objective criteria
- Carve-out possible with conditions
- Limited cooperation language

### 45-59 CONCERN
- **Carve-out restricted by exclusivity clause with only consent-based mechanism**
- **Carve-out triggers pricing penalty scoped to affected services**
- Significant barriers to unbundling
- No cooperation commitment

### Below 45 RED FLAG
- Carve-out explicitly prohibited OR exclusivity with no carve-out mechanism
- Blanket contract repricing triggered by any carve-out
- Pricing penalty eliminates economic benefit of carve-out
- No transition support

## Quote Patterns

**✅ POSITIVE:**
- "shall have the right to carve out any pharmacy benefit service"
- "without penalty or revision to remaining service fees"
- "cooperate fully with any carve-out transition"
- "no exclusivity"
- "PBM acknowledges it cannot match" + deemed approved if no response → Score 4

**⚠️ WATCH FOR:**
- "Subject to mutual agreement" → Score 3 (single-gate consent)
- "As agreed to by PBM" after PBM declines → Score 2 (double-gate)
- "May affect pricing" language → determines 7.3 score
- Narrow carve-out scope

**🔴 RED FLAG:**
- **"Exclusive pharmacy benefits manager"** with no carve-out mechanism → Score 0 on 7.2
- **"If pharmacy benefits are carved out the ASO fees quoted above may be revised"** → Blanket repricing, Score 0 on 7.3
- "Client violates exclusivity requirements" as Pricing Event triggering all financial terms → Score 0 on 7.3

## Reference Scores

| Contract | Score | Key Factors |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Disclosed Rx | 21 | Exclusivity required; carve-out consent-gated with preconditions (score 2 on 7.2); silent on penalties |
| **Smith Health** | **5** | **Exclusivity required; no carve-out mechanism** (score 1 on 7.2 — silent); no penalties (silent) |
| **Optum Rx** | **55** | **Pricing penalty** for carve-out: "ASO fees may be revised"; no explicit carve-out rights |
| **LucyRx** | **0** | Blanket exclusivity; carve-outs prohibited (score 0 on 7.2); blanket repricing of all financial terms (score 0 on 7.3) |

## Calibration Notes

**Key Insight — Issue 7.2 Consent Spectrum:** The fiduciary quality of a carve-out right depends on whether the Plan Sponsor can act unilaterally or must obtain PBM approval. Consent-based carve-outs (scores 2–3) are weak because the PBM can simply refuse. Outcome-triggered carve-outs (score 4) are strong because the right activates automatically once objective criteria are met — the PBM cannot veto access to a superior clinical program by withholding consent.

**PEPM Guarantee Justification:** PBMs that require exclusivity to backstop PEPM performance guarantees have a legitimate business rationale. The score 4 model language converts this from a blanket lockout into a performance-accountability mechanism: the PBM holds the scope when it can perform, and the Plan Sponsor can access better programs when the PBM cannot match them. This aligns fiduciary obligation with PBM commercial interest.

**Issue 7.3 — Proportionate vs. Blanket Penalty:** Score 0 applies only to blanket repricing of unrelated terms (e.g., LucyRx's Exhibit C triggering repricing of *all* financial terms). A pricing adjustment limited to the carved-out service component is proportionate and scores 2. A PBM that says "carve out specialty and we'll recalculate the specialty PEPM guarantee component" is different from one that says "carve out anything and we'll reprice everything."

**Carve-Out Importance:** The ability to carve out specialty pharmacy alone can save 15-25% on specialty spend. For members with autoimmune conditions or other complex clinical needs, inability to access best-in-class disease management programs is both a cost and quality-of-care issue. Barriers to carve-out represent significant hidden costs and potential fiduciary exposure.

# PROVISION #8: LOWEST NET COST AND CLINICAL EFFECTIVENESS

## Definition
PBM commits to managing formulary for lowest net cost to Plan and participants, not to maximize rebate revenue.

## Model Language
> PBM shall manage the formulary to achieve the lowest net cost for the Plan and its participants, not to maximize rebate revenue.
> 
> PBM shall not prefer higher-cost branded drugs over lower-cost generics or biosimilars for the purpose of generating rebates.
> 
> PBM shall provide the rationale for any formulary placement in which a higher-cost drug receives favorable treatment over a lower-cost clinically appropriate alternative.

**CAA 2026 Alignment:** §6701 requires PBMs to report on the rationale for higher-cost drugs receiving favorable formulary treatment. This is the primary LNC verification mechanism — not P&T committee minutes. Plan sponsors can cross-reference the formulary rationale report against the LNC attestation.

## Scoring Thresholds

### 90-100 EXCELLENT
- Explicit "lowest net cost" commitment
- Anti-rebate-chasing language
- Generic/biosimilar preference stated
- Therapeutic alternatives considered
- Patient cost-sharing considered in net cost calculation
- Formulary placement rationale provided for higher-cost drugs with favorable treatment

### 75-89 GOOD
- "Cost-effective care" commitment
- Active cost management programs
- P&T committee with clinical focus
- Missing explicit anti-rebate-chasing language

### 60-74 ACCEPTABLE
- General cost management language
- Standard formulary management
- Some clinical programs
- No explicit lowest net cost commitment

### 45-59 CONCERN
- Formulary optimized for rebates
- Limited cost commitment
- Rebate guarantees drive formulary
- Patient cost-sharing not addressed

### Below 45 RED FLAG
- **STRUCTURAL:** Rebate-driven formulary is business model
- No cost commitment
- **Rebate protection clause** penalizes cost optimization
- Explicit rebate chasing

## Quote Patterns

**✅ POSITIVE:**
- "lowest net cost for the Plan and its participants"
- "shall not prefer higher-cost branded drugs over lower-cost generics"
- "not to maximize rebate revenue"
- "therapeutic alternatives"

**⚠️ WATCH FOR:**
- "Cost-effective" without definition
- Rebate guarantees tied to formulary compliance
- Generic programs without biosimilar mention

**🔴 RED FLAG:**
- **"We reserve the right to adjust our rebate guarantee if changes made to our PDL for the purpose of achieving lower net drug cost... result in significant reductions to the rebate level"** → Optum's actual language
- Rebate protection language
- Formulary compliance requirements for rebate receipt
- No cost commitment

## Reference Scores

| Contract | Score | Key Factors |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Disclosed Rx | 85 | "Lowest net cost," Savings Program, explicit commitment |
| Smith Health | 75 | "Cost-effective care," active programs, P&T committee; missing explicit anti-rebate-chasing |
| **Optum Rx** | **30** | **STRUCTURAL:** Rebate protection clause explicitly penalizes cost optimization; no lowest net cost commitment |

## Calibration Notes

**Key Insight:** Optum's score (30) is driven by the explicit **rebate protection clause** which is the *opposite* of a lowest net cost commitment. They're saying: "If you try to lower costs by changing the formulary, we may reduce your rebates."

**This is Anti-Fiduciary:** A fiduciary-aligned PBM should *want* lower costs even if it means lower rebates. The rebate protection clause reveals that Optum's incentives are misaligned with participant interests.

# PROVISION #9: TERMINATION WITHOUT PENALTY AND CLEAN EXIT RIGHTS

## Definition
Plan can terminate without economic penalty and PBM cooperates fully with transition to successor.

## Model Language
> Plan Sponsor shall retain the right to terminate this Agreement without punitive penalties upon PBM failure to comply with fiduciary alignment, transparency, or disclosure obligations.
> 
> All rebates earned through the termination date shall be paid in full per the standard payment schedule, regardless of reason for termination.
> 
> PBM shall cooperate promptly with transition activities, data transfer, and continuity of care. All Plan data shall be returned within thirty (30) days of termination in industry-standard format at no additional charge.

## Scoring Thresholds

### 90-100 EXCELLENT
- No penalty termination (60-90 days notice)
- Rebates continue through termination date
- No rebate forfeiture
- Full cooperation commitment
- Detailed transition data provision
- Continuity of care provisions

### 75-89 GOOD
- Clean exit (60-90 days notice)
- Rebate continuation
- Data transfer
- Missing detailed cooperation language

### 60-74 ACCEPTABLE
- Termination allowed
- Some conditions
- Basic data transfer
- Longer notice periods (90-120 days)

### 45-59 CONCERN
- Significant exit barriers
- Long notice periods (180+ days)
- Partial rebate forfeiture
- Limited transition support

### Below 45 RED FLAG
- **STRUCTURAL:** Termination penalties are business model (lock-in)
- Termination fees
- **100% rebate forfeiture**
- Economic penalties designed to trap Plan
- No transition cooperation
- Extended notice requirements (12+ months)

## Quote Patterns

**✅ POSITIVE:**
- "terminate... upon sixty (60) days written notice without penalty"
- "receive all accrued rebates through the termination date"
- "cooperate fully with transition activities"
- "provide all claims history, formulary information, prior authorization records"
- "continuity of care"

**⚠️ WATCH FOR:**
- Longer notice periods (90 days acceptable, 180+ concerning)
- "Reasonably requested data" (narrower than "all data")
- Transition cooperation implied but not stated

**🔴 RED FLAG:**
- **"If Customer terminates before 03/31/2029, United retains 100% of all pending and future rebates"** → Optum's actual language
- **"Repay prorated portion of the credit"** → Additional exit penalty
- "Early termination penalty"
- "Amounts owed shall be accelerated"
- Extended notice requirements designed to deter exit

## Reference Scores

| Contract | Score | Key Factors |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Disclosed Rx | 95 | Dual termination options, "cooperate fully," detailed transition data, continuity of care |
| Smith Health | 90 | 60-day exit, rebate continuation, data transfer; missing cooperation detail |
| **Optum Rx** | **15** | **STRUCTURAL:** 100% rebate forfeiture on early termination; 3-year lock-in; admin credit repayment; designed to trap Plan |

## Calibration Notes

**Key Insight:** Optum's score (15) is the lowest in the comparison set because the termination penalties are **the most punitive we've seen**:
- 100% forfeiture of pending rebates
- 100% forfeiture of **future** rebates (not just accrued)
- Repayment of admin credits
- 3-year lock-in period

**Lock-In Economics:** The termination penalty creates a financial trap. Even if a better PBM arrangement is available, the forfeiture cost may exceed the savings benefith is exactly the point.

**This is Structural:** Lock-in provisions are part of traditional PBM business models designed to reduce competitive pressure. They cannot be negotiated to zero because they protect the PBM's revenue base.

# PROVISION #10: ADMINISTRATIVE FEE VERIFICATION AND BENCHMARKING

## Definition
Fees are transparent, verifiable, and benchmarked against PBM's book of business.

## Model Language
> PBM administrative fees shall be transparent, fully disclosed, and verifiable. All fee components shall be itemized, including administrative fees, pass-through costs, and third-party charges. No hidden fees or undisclosed charges shall apply.
> 
> PBM shall certify that administrative fees constitute bona fide service fees and represent the sole source of revenue derived from the Plan relationship. PBM receives no spread, rebate retention, or other compensation beyond the disclosed administrative fees.

**CAA 2026 Alignment:** ERISA §408(b)(2)(B) requires that PBM compensation qualify as "bona fide service fees." Explicit use of this statutory term strengthens the ERISA cross-reference.
> 
> PBM shall provide annual benchmarking reports showing Plan's PEPM costs relative to PBM's book of business, including percentile positioning, comparison to plans of similar size and utilization profile, and improvement targets for the following twelve months.

## Scoring Thresholds

### 90-100 EXCELLENT
- Fees fully disclosed in contract
- Fee structure clear and understandable
- "Bona fide service fees" or "sole source of revenue" language
- Third-party fees disclosed (e.g., rebate aggregator)
- Annual fee verification mechanism
- Benchmarking against book of business

### 75-89 GOOD
- Fees fully disclosed
- Fee structure clear
- "Sole revenue" language
- Third-party fees disclosed
- Annual verification
- Missing benchmarking

### 60-74 ACCEPTABLE
- Fees disclosed
- Fee structure understood
- Some revenue attestation
- No benchmarking
- Third-party fees unclear

### 45-59 CONCERN
- Fees disclosed but structure complex
- Multiple fee types unclear
- **Fees funded by retained rebates** (obscures true cost)
- No "sole revenue" attestation
- No verification mechanism

### Below 45 RED FLAG
- Hidden fees
- Fee structure opaque
- Multiple undisclosed revenue sources
- No verification mechanism
- "Other fees may apply" language

## Quote Patterns

**✅ POSITIVE:**
- "Administrative Fee: $X.XX PMPM" or "$X.XX per Claim"
- "Administrative Fees are the only consideration [PBM] receives"
- "sole source of revenue from Client will be its Administrative Fee"
- "report containing evidence that the only revenue [PBM] receives are those fees disclosed"
- Named rebate aggregator with fee structure

**⚠️ WATCH FOR:**
- Variable fee structures (% of savings)
- Bundled vs. unbundled fees
- Third-party fees embedded vs. disclosed separately

**🔴 RED FLAG:**
- **"Administrative Fee Credit: Under this arrangement, Rebates retained by United are used to lower the medical administration fee"** → Optum's language (credit funded by retained rebates)
- "Fees subject to adjustment"
- "Other fees may apply"
- Fee structure unclear
- No "sole revenue" attestation

## Reference Scores

| Contract | Score | Key Factors |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| Disclosed Rx | 90 | Clear per-claim + savings fee, "sole source," aggregator transparency; missing benchmarking |
| Smith Health | 90 | Clear PMPM fees, annual verification report, "only consideration"; missing benchmarking |
| **Optum Rx** | **45** | Fees disclosed ($60 PEPM + $20 commission) but $30 credit funded by retained rebates obscures true cost; multiple undisclosed revenue sources |

## Calibration Notes

**Key Insight:** Optum's fees ARE disclosed, which prevents a score below 45. However, the "$30 PEPM Administrative Fee Credit funded by retained rebates" creates opacity're giving back a portion of what they're keeping from rebates, which obscures the true fee structure.

**True Cost Calculation:** If Optum retains $100K in rebates and returns $30 PEPM ($64K) as an "admin credit," the net fee is really higher than disclosed because the "credit" is funded by Plan money that should have been passed through.

# FUTURE DEVELOPMENT: PBM ACCOUNTABILITY INDEX

## Vision

As Contract X-Ray analyzes more contracts, the accumulated data enables a **public PBM Accountability Index** market-wide comparison of PBM contract quality that creates transparency pressure and helps employers make informed decisions.

**Concept:**
- Standardized scores across all analyzed PBMs
- Public ranking by overall score and by provision
- Market percentiles (e.g., "This PBM is in the 25th percentile on Termination provisions")
- Trend tracking as PBMs update contract language

## Path to Index

1. **Current:** Three calibrated contracts (Disclosed Rx, Smith, Optum)
2. **Next:** Add 3-5 more contracts across PBM types
3. **Threshold:** ~10 contracts enables meaningful market comparison
4. **Index Launch:** Public report with anonymized (or named) PBM rankings

## Scoring Framework Evolution

The current rubric uses qualitative thresholds. Future versions will implement a **weighted, clause-based scoring system** for greater consistency and automation potential:

**Tier 1: Disqualifying Clauses (Heavy Weight)**
Presence triggers large automatic deductions:
- Explicit spread pricing admission → -40 to -50
- 100% rebate forfeiture → -40 to -50
- Owns mail AND specialty pharmacy → -35 to -45

**Tier 2: Protection Clauses (Moderate Weight)**
Absence triggers moderate deductions:
- No ERISA acknowledgment → -10 to -15
- No carve-out rights → -15 to -20
- No extrapolation → -10 to -15

**Tier 3: Excellence Clauses (Lower Weight)**
Presence triggers small bonuses:
- Aggregator transparency → +5
- Benchmarking reports → +5

This evolution enables:
- **Consistency:** Same clause always produces same impact
- **Comparability:** Scores are directly comparable across contracts
- **Automation:** AI can detect clauses and apply weights
- **Defensibility:** Methodology is transparent and replicable
