Contract X-Ray / CAA 2026 Readiness

The compliance work CAA 2026 expects starts with the contract.

How Contract X-Ray maps to the new disclosure and oversight obligations, and where the PBM market is moving in response.

01 · What CAA 2026 changes

From passive disclosure to active oversight.

CAA 2026 amended ERISA §408(b)(2)(B) to expand the scope of compensation and rebate disclosures PBMs must provide to plan sponsors, and to sharpen the plan sponsor’s obligation to verify what is disclosed. The shift is from passive receipt to active oversight. Receiving the disclosure is not the obligation. Confirming that the disclosure is complete and accurate, and acting on what it shows, is.

The proposed implementing rule from the Department of Labor adds detail on disclosure format, scope, and the plan sponsor’s verification responsibilities. Nautilus submitted a formal comment letter on the proposed rule.

Statute P.L. 119-75, enacted February 3, 2026
Amends ERISA §408(b)(2)(B)
Proposed rule DOL RIN 1210-AB37, published January 30, 2026

02 · Where the contract carries the burden

Four provisions where the contract determines what the plan sponsor can actually do.

CAA 2026 compliance work happens in two places: in the contract and in operational practice. The contract sets the authority the plan sponsor has to verify, audit, and act. Four of the ten Contract X-Ray provisions intersect directly with the new obligations.

  • P5

    Audit Rights & Verification

    Active oversight depends on the contractual authority to audit. Restricted audit clauses (pre-approved firms only, blackout windows, no extrapolation, single annual look) constrain what the plan sponsor can verify and what evidence supports the oversight obligation.

  • P4

    Data Ownership & Rights

    Verification requires data. Contracts that restrict access to claims, eligibility, or rebate data limit the plan sponsor’s ability to confirm that disclosures match operational reality.

  • P3

    Rebate & Manufacturer Revenue

    Expanded disclosure scope under CAA 2026 covers manufacturer-derived revenue. Contracts silent on full rebate pass-through, or that retain categories of manufacturer revenue outside the rebate definition, create attestation gaps.

  • P10

    Administrative Fee Transparency

    All compensation sources, direct and indirect, fall under the expanded disclosure regime. Contracts with aggregated or non-itemized fee structures create disclosure friction the plan sponsor must resolve.

03 · What a Contract X-Ray analysis tells you

A specific read on where your contract stands.

A Contract X-Ray analysis returns a score and citation for each of the four CAA-relevant provisions plus the other six. The output identifies where contract language supports compliance work, where it constrains it, and where the contract is silent.

For plan sponsors at renewal, the analysis becomes the input to a CAA readiness negotiation pack: specific provisions ranked, specific language proposed, and specific evidence on how the current contract compares to a reference set drawn from approximately 20 distinct PBMs.

04 · How the market is responding

PBMs are building forward. The first public benchmark publishes in July.

The plan sponsor view of CAA 2026 readiness is one side of the conversation. The PBM view is the other. PBMs that recognize the market is moving toward CAA-aligned contracts are using the Contract X-Ray framework to build forward rather than defend backward. Five to ten PBMs are expected to be working actively with the methodology by mid-2026.

The market signal is about to become public.

05 · What this is not

Three things the framework does not claim to be.

  • Not legal compliance advice. The framework evaluates whether contract language supports CAA-related obligations. Whether your plan is in compliance with CAA 2026 is a determination for your ERISA counsel.
  • Not a DOL filing or attestation. A Contract X-Ray report is an analytical document for the fiduciary process. It is not a regulatory submission and it does not substitute for any disclosure or attestation the law requires.
  • Not a guarantee. A contract that scores well on the four CAA-relevant provisions still requires operational practice that matches what the contract says. Strong contract language and weak operational practice produce the same compliance exposure as weak contract language.
